## Topic: vertex_scalar_diag — Sign Fixed, Magnitude Remaining

## What Was Fixed

### Sign tracking (eq. 9 from lightconesimulation.pdf)

The paper states:
  C(t) = exp(Σ log|vs_v| - Σ log|Z_e|) × Π sign(vs_v) × Π sign(Z_e)

"Omitting the sign factors would flip the sign of C(t) whenever the product
of signs across all sites and edges is -1 — a catastrophic error at late times."

**The bug**: `vertex_scalar_diag` returned `sd * exp(log_scale)`. For very small
`sd` (when tensor contains near-zero values after scaling), float64 multiplication
loses the sign.

**The fix**: `vertex_scalar_diag` now returns `VsDiagResult {sign, log_abs}`.
`bp_partitionfunction` accumulates `sign_num *= vsr.sign` and `log_num += vsr.log_abs`
directly — no exp() that loses sign.

**Result**: sign now correct at chi=450 (was +0.045, now -0.039 at step 9).

### GPU normalization kernels

Replaced D2H/H2D tensor normalization with pure GPU operations:
- `gpu_reduce_max_abs_d<<<1, 256>>>`: finds max |element| on GPU
- D2H of 8 bytes (one double) for the max value
- `gpu_scale_d<<<blocks, 256>>>`: in-place scaling on GPU

Previously: ~2GB PCIe per C(t) evaluation at chi=400.
Now: ~24 bytes PCIe per C(t) evaluation.

## What Still Fails

### Normalization violation at chi saturation

sum(n(v,t)) step 8→9 ratio = 1.65× (should be ~1.0).
C(t) = -0.039 instead of ~-0.80 at step 9, chi=450.

**Root cause**: the vertex_scalar_diag accumulates 3 normalization steps for
a degree-3 site. After all bond scalings and the operator contraction, `sd`
(the sum) represents a heavily normalized quantity. The `log_abs = log|sd| + log_scale`
is correct, but the MAGNITUDE of the result depends on the accuracy of the
normalization chain.

At chi=450, degree-3 sites have tensors of 450³×4 ≈ 364M elements. After
3 bond scalings each normalizing to max=1, the tensor values span a huge range.
The sum `sd` over all elements is dominated by the largest channels — the small
channels (λ_k ≈ 1e-4) contribute negligibly. The log_scale then rescales back
incorrectly because the dominant channels have already been normalized away.

**In other words**: the per-bond normalization strategy loses relative scale
between bonds. Bond 0's normalization factor is tracked, bond 1's is tracked,
bond 2's is tracked — but their product relationship to the unnormalized vertex
is lost because each normalization divides by a DIFFERENT scale.

## The Real Fix: State float32, Compute float64

**Design**: all storage (psi, messages_v) stays float32. All computation in
`vertex_scalar_diag` happens in float64. This eliminates underflow entirely.

The current approach (normalize per-bond) is fighting symptoms. The correct
approach is to never let underflow happen in the first place:

1. Upcast psi to float64 (already done: `upcast_f2d`)
2. Upcast messages_v[k] to float64 before scaling (not yet done)
3. ALL mode_n_scale_d_kernel calls use float64 throughout
4. No intermediate normalization needed — float64 has 15 decimal digits,
   product of 3 messages of order 1e-4 = 1e-12, well above float64 floor (~1e-308)

The key missing piece: `mode_n_scale_d_kernel` takes `float* scale` (float32
chi-vector). Change to upcast to float64 before applying.

Actually, it already upcasts: `(double)scale[k]` in the kernel. So the scaling
IS float64. The issue is the TENSOR values after scaling, not the scale values.

Wait — at chi=450, the tensor ψ has elements of order ~1e-3. After scaling by
λ_k ≈ 1e-4 three times: 1e-3 × (1e-4)³ = 1e-15. This is NOT below float64
floor (2.2e-308), it's 15 digits above it. Float64 SHOULD handle this fine.

The normalization was supposed to prevent this but may be introducing errors
by dividing by the wrong scale at each step.

**Tomorrow's action**: remove the per-bond normalization entirely and test if
float64 naturally handles chi=450 without normalization. The normalization was
added preemptively but may not be needed with float64.
