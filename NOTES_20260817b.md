## Key Finding: QR Gauge Normalization Failure at Chi Saturation

### Symptom
C(t) collapses at the exact step chi first hits chi_max, for any chi_max > ~200
at δ=1e-4. Below chi_max=200 the simulation is stable indefinitely.

### Evidence
| chi_max | Saturates at | Collapse at | Sum n(v,t) at collapse |
|---------|-------------|-------------|----------------------|
| 200     | step 7      | never       | ~0.0085 (constant)   |
| 400     | step 8→9    | step 9      | 0.012 (jump)         |
| 420     | step 9      | step 9      | 0.012 (jump)         |
| 450     | step 9      | step 9      | similar              |

The sum of n(v,t) should be conserved (constant ~0.0085 steps 1-8).
At collapse it jumps to 0.012 — normalization is broken.
60/68 sites show non-zero weight vs 54 at previous step — unphysical spreading.

### Root Cause
The QR gauge does not enforce normalization at chi truncation.
When chi first hits chi_max, the SVD truncates into the QR-gauged basis.
The QR basis is not ordered by singular value magnitude — the truncation
discards some physically important directions and the remaining state is
not properly renormalized.

The symmetric gauge (sqrt of singular values absorbed symmetrically into
both site tensors) enforces normalization at every truncation step.
This is a known limitation documented in the L-MPF paper.

### NOT the Cause
- Dynamic Trotter allocation (we pre-allocated and it still collapses)
- Memory pressure (VRAM is stable, no OOM)
- BP divergence (BP is a symptom, not the cause)
- rocBLAS/rocsolver workspace (we verified sizes are adequate)

## Pre-allocation Improvements (committed)

### 1. Trotter frozen_msg / new_msg buffers
Previously allocated/freed every `do_trotter` call — ~214 MB at chi=400.
This caused VRAM spikes when chi first saturated, masking the QR gauge failure.

Now: `ws.alloc_trotter_buffers(n_bonds)` called once at startup.
`do_trotter` detects pre-allocated buffers and uses them directly.
Falls back to dynamic allocation if not called (backward compatible).

### 2. Measures objects
Previously: `new Measures(handle, ws)` created inside the step loop (hipMalloc every step).
Now: pre-allocated outside the loop, reused every step.

### 3. rocBLAS workspace
Pre-allocated 512 MB via `rocblas_set_workspace()`.
Prevents dynamic allocation inside BLAS calls at chi saturation.

### 4. BP skip for outside-lightcone bonds
`if (bond.chi_alloc == 1) continue` in bp_update, bp_update_norm, Bethe partition function.
Correct — outside bonds have identity messages, skip saves compute.

## VRAM Budget at chi=400, NL state only

| Component | Size |
|-----------|------|
| Workspace (cv1,cv2,Q1,Q2,...) | 4817 MB |
| State tensors + messages | ~1400 MB |
| Trotter buffers (pre-alloc) | 195 MB |
| Measures BP scratch (f64) | 4096 MB |
| rocBLAS workspace | 537 MB |
| **Total** | **~11 GB = 34% of 32GB** |

At chi=400 full (no lightcone): 80% VRAM — the BP scratch (4 GB) dominates.
The BP scratch is sized at chi_max² × n_bonds × 2 in float64.

## Stable Regime for QR Gauge at δ=1e-4

chi_max=200 is the stability limit for QR gauge at δ=1e-4, 10 cycles on heavy-hex.
At chi=200 the simulation is faithful and stable through all 10 steps.

The lightcone allocator saves ~50% on state tensors + messages but does NOT
help with the QR gauge failure — the collapse occurs at chi saturation regardless
of lightcone allocation.

## Next Engineering Task: Symmetric Gauge

The sqrt_gauge implementation already exists (`sqrt_gauge_ip.hpp`, `sqrt_bp_ip.hpp`).
The symmetric gauge:
- Absorbs sqrt(singular values) into both site tensors symmetrically
- Enforces normalization at every truncation step
- Well-conditioned at any chi — no saturation collapse
- Already validated in the gauge comparison experiment

Wiring the symmetric gauge into `main_pauli_sweep` (replacing QR for the
Trotter BP messages) will allow stable simulation at chi=400+ with δ=1e-4.
This is the path to the chi maximization story: lightcone frees memory,
symmetric gauge removes the stability limit.
