\begin{abstract}
We extend CppSim, a high-performance C++/HIP tensor network simulator,
to the heavy-hex lattice
geometry used in superconducting quantum processors.
The heavy-hex graph — a honeycomb of degree-2 and degree-3 sites with
a natural 3-color gate schedule — is implemented as a drop-in grid class
with no changes to the gate application or belief propagation (BP) kernels.
We simulate the isotropic Heisenberg model
$H = J\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}(\mathbf{X}_i\mathbf{X}_j +
\mathbf{Y}_i\mathbf{Y}_j + \mathbf{Z}_i\mathbf{Z}_j)$
in the Heisenberg picture using Pauli transfer matrices,
and study the autocorrelation $C(t) = \langle Z_c(t)Z_c(0)\rangle$
and operator lightcone on the \texttt{heavy\_hex\_3x3} graph (68 sites, 76 bonds).
We demonstrate chi convergence at $\chi=200$ ($|\mathrm{error}|<10^{-4}$,
reference $\chi=430$) on a 32\,GB GPU, and show a $\sim$10$\times$
speedup over a Julia/AMDGPU.jl reference at saturated bond dimension.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Heavy-hex is the lattice topology used in superconducting quantum processors
\cite{heavyhex-topology}. Each qubit site has degree 2 or 3, and the graph admits
a natural 3-coloring of its bonds, enabling parallel gate application within
each color class.

Classical tensor network (TN) simulation of quantum circuits on heavy-hex graphs
serves two purposes: (i) validation of quantum computations for small system sizes
where exact simulation is feasible
\cite{tindall2023gauging,tindall2024eagle,tindall2025disordered,rudolph2025simulating},
and (ii) computation of noise-free reference values for quantum error mitigation
protocols such as the Multi-Product Formula (MPF) \cite{mpf-ref}.

In this paper we show that CppSim \cite{dalberto2026cppsim}, originally developed for
rectangular 2D Ising dynamics, extends naturally to heavy-hex topology with
minimal code changes. The hot-path kernels — gate application, BP,
workspace management — are topology-agnostic and require no modification.
Only the grid construction and Hamiltonian gate matrix need to be defined
for the new geometry.

\section{Heavy-Hex Lattice and Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:grid}

We describe the graph topology, its implementation in CppSim, and the
Hamiltonian gate matrix used for time evolution.

\subsection{Graph structure}

The \texttt{heavy\_hex\_$n_x \times n_y$} graph has:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $(n_x+1)(n_y+1)$ \emph{junction} sites (degree 3)
    \item remaining \emph{arm} sites (degree 2) connecting adjacent junctions
    \item $2n_x n_y + n_x + n_y$ bonds total
\end{itemize}

For $n_x = n_y = 3$: 16 junction sites, 52 arm sites, 76 bonds.
The graph is bipartite and 3-edge-colorable — bonds partition into 3 color
classes of sizes 25, 25, 26, corresponding to the natural gate layers
of the hardware gate schedule.

Site tensors have shapes $[\chi^2, D]$ (arm) and $[\chi^3, D]$ (junction),
where $D=4$ is the Pauli basis dimension $\{I, X, Y, Z\}$.
The memory cost is dominated by junction sites:
$16 \times \chi^3 \times D \times 4$\,B (float32).

\subsection{Implementation}

The \texttt{FlatHeavyHexGrid} class inherits from \texttt{Grid} and populates
the same \texttt{Site}/\texttt{Bond}/\texttt{Gate} data structures used by the
rectangular grid. The color class assignment follows the hardware gate schedule
(JSON layers 0, 1, 2). No changes are required in \texttt{gate.hpp},
\texttt{measures.hpp}, or \texttt{workspace.hpp} beyond parametrizing the
workspace by \texttt{max\_degree=3} instead of 4.

\subsection{Hamiltonian and gate matrix}

We simulate the isotropic Heisenberg (XXX) model:
\begin{equation}
    H = J \sum_{\langle ij \rangle} \bigl(X_i X_j + Y_i Y_j + Z_i Z_j\bigr)
\end{equation}
with uniform coupling $J = 1$ and time step $\delta t = 0.1$.

In the Heisenberg picture with Pauli basis, the two-site gate
$U = e^{-i\theta(X\otimes X + Y\otimes Y + Z\otimes Z)}$
($\theta = J\delta t$) is represented by a $16\times 16$ real
Pauli transfer matrix (PTM) with block structure:
\begin{align}
    \{II, XX, YY, ZZ\} &\to \text{invariant (diagonal = 1)} \\
    \{IX, XI, YZ, ZY\} &\to \text{$4\times 4$ mixing block} \\
    \{IY, YI, XZ, ZX\} &\to \text{$4\times 4$ mixing block} \\
    \{IZ, ZI, XY, YX\} &\to \text{$4\times 4$ mixing block}
\end{align}
with elements $\alpha = \cos^2(2\theta)$, $\beta = \sin^2(2\theta)$,
$\gamma = \sin(2\theta)\cos(2\theta)$.
This was verified against a Julia reference implementation.

\section{Autocorrelation and Chi Convergence}
\label{sec:ct}

We initialize the Heisenberg-picture operator as $Z$ at a single center site
(site 13, a degree-3 junction at the graph midpoint) and $I$ elsewhere.
The autocorrelation $C(t) = \langle Z_c(t) Z_c(0) \rangle$ is computed
via the Bethe approximation \cite{tindall2023gauging,evenbly2026loop} after each Trotter step.
We study convergence with respect to the bond dimension $\chi$ by running
nine simulations with $\chi \in \{50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, 430\}$
over 30 Trotter steps at cutoff $10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Convergence with $\chi$}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{ct_convergence.png}
    \caption{Autocorrelation $C(t) = \langle Z_c(t) Z_c(0) \rangle$ for
    $\chi = 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, 430$ over 30 Trotter steps.
    The oscillatory behaviour is a genuine quantum recurrence of the XXX model
    on the finite heavy-hex graph. Curves for $\chi \geq 150$ are visually
    indistinguishable, indicating convergence.}
    \label{fig:ct}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{ct_error.png}
    \caption{C(t) truncation error $|C(t,\chi) - C(t,\chi=430)|$ vs cycle
    for each $\chi$ value. Errors onset when the bond dimension first saturates
    at the cap (visible as the left edge of each curve), then grow as more bonds
    are affected. At cycle 10: $\chi=400$ gives error $3\times10^{-6}$,
    $\chi=150$ gives $1.7\times10^{-4}$.}
    \label{fig:error}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:ct} shows $C(t)$ for $\chi = 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300,
350, 400, 430$ over 30 Trotter steps.
The result converges to $\lesssim 10^{-4}$ at $\chi = 150$ and to
$3\times10^{-6}$ at $\chi = 400$ (reference $\chi=430$),
indicating that $\chi_{\max} = 200$ is sufficient for
$|{\rm error}| < 10^{-4}$ on this graph at 10 cycles.

The non-monotonic behaviour of $C(t)$ — a decrease followed by partial
recovery — is a genuine quantum recurrence of the XXX model on the degree-3
lattice, confirmed by its independence from $\chi$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:error}).

\subsection{Operator lightcone}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{lightcone_sites_spatial_site13.png}
    \caption{Non-identity Pauli weight $n(v,t)$ at each site of the
    heavy\_hex\_3x3 graph over 20 Trotter steps, initialised at site 13
    (mid-graph degree-3 junction, marked cyan). The operator spreads radially
    outward with 3-fold hexagonal symmetry, reaching the graph boundary

    \label{fig:lightcone}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:lightcone} shows the non-identity Pauli weight $n(v,t)$
at each site for 20 cycles. The operator spreads radially outward
from the center junction, following the 3-fold symmetry of the hexagonal
lattice. The wavefront reaches the boundary sites by cycle 17,
defining the computational window within which the simulation is exact
regardless of $\chi$ truncation at the boundary.

\section{Performance}
\label{sec:perf}

We report wall-clock times on a 32\,GB GPU using Householder QR and float32 arithmetic.
All timings are per Trotter cycle at saturated bond dimension.

Figures~\ref{fig:chi_growth} and \ref{fig:timing} show bond dimension growth

The dominant cost is the Householder QR decomposition
($\mathcal{O}(m n^2)$ where $m = \chi^2 D$ for degree-3 sites),
scaling approximately as $\chi^3$.

At saturated bond dimension ($\chi = 430$, cycles 8--10), CppSim achieves
a $\sim$10$\times$ speedup over the Julia/AMDGPU.jl reference implementation
on the same GPU. Both use Householder QR and
float32 arithmetic. The speedup arises from:
(i) zero GPU memory allocation during simulation (pre-allocated workspace),
(ii) direct HIP/rocBLAS calls without framework dispatch overhead, and
(iii) on-device \texttt{pseudo\_sqrt} kernel eliminating CPU--GPU transfers.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have demonstrated that CppSim, a C++/HIP tensor network simulator,
extends to the heavy-hex lattice with minimal code changes.
The topology-agnostic gate and BP kernels accept any \texttt{Grid}-derived
class; the heavy-hex extension requires only a new site/bond/color construction
and a $16\times16$ Pauli transfer matrix for the XXX Hamiltonian.

On a 32\,GB GPU, the $3\times3$ heavy-hex graph (68 sites, 76 bonds)
is simulated with bond dimension $\chi \leq 430$.
The autocorrelation $C(t)$ converges at $\chi = 200$
($|\mathrm{error}| < 10^{-4}$ relative to $\chi=430$) for 10 Trotter cycles.
The non-monotonic behaviour of $C(t)$ — a quantum recurrence of the
isotropic Heisenberg model on the finite graph — is confirmed independent
of $\chi$ and is therefore a physical prediction of the simulation.
We demonstrate a $\sim$10$\times$ speedup over a Julia/AMDGPU.jl reference
\cite{rudolph2025simulating} at saturated bond dimension.

The operator lightcone, computed as a byproduct with no additional cost,
visualizes operator spreading on the hexagonal lattice and defines the
computational window within which the simulation is exact.

Future work will extend to larger graphs on higher-memory hardware,
and implement interleaved BP to match the online approach of
\cite{tindall2023gauging} and eliminate BP trap effects.
